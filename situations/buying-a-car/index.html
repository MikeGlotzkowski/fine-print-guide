Buying a car

You need insurance before you drive a car you own. The dealer or seller will often ask for proof.

At a glance

  1. Have liability coverage in place Required by law
  2. If you finance or lease: collision, comprehensive and gap Often required by someone else
  3. Choose limits and deductibles Optional
  4. List every regular driver Often required by someone else

  1. 1 Have liability coverage in place

    Required by law

    Every state except New Hampshire requires it. Check your state's minimum, then consider higher limits.

    Read more: Car insurance

  2. 2 If you finance or lease: collision, comprehensive and gap

    Often required by someone else

    The lender will require collision and comprehensive. Consider gap insurance: if the car is totaled early, it pays what you still owe beyond the car's value.

  3. 3 Choose limits and deductibles

    Optional

    Higher deductibles lower your premium, but you pay more after a claim. Higher liability and uninsured motorist limits cost more but protect more.

  4. 4 List every regular driver

    Often required by someone else

    Anyone in your household who drives the car regularly usually has to be listed. Leaving someone off can lead to a denied claim.

Things that often surprise people

  • Insurance generally follows the car: if a friend borrows it and crashes, your policy pays first.
  • Driving for a rideshare or delivery app isn't covered by a personal policy without an add-on.
  • Hitting a deer is a comprehensive claim, not a collision claim.

General information, not advice. Policies differ by insurer and by state. What your own policy says is what counts, so use this site to know what to look for and what to ask.
