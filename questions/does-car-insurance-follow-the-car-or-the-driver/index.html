Question

Does car insurance follow the car or the driver?

Short answer

Mostly the car. If you lend your car to a friend and they crash, your policy usually pays first. Their own car insurance may then pay on top if your limits run out.

Lending your car

  • Your policy usually covers someone who drives your car with your permission, now and then.
  • A claim goes on your record and can raise your price, even though you weren't driving.
  • Someone who lives with you and drives your car regularly usually has to be listed on your policy. Leaving them off can lead to a denied claim.

Borrowing someone else's car

The owner's policy pays first. If the damage is bigger than their limits, your own liability coverage usually adds to it. Your collision coverage usually doesn't pay to repair a car you borrowed regularly, so check before relying on it.

When the rules change

Some states and some policies differ, and many policies exclude drivers you name as excluded. Rental cars and cars used for deliveries or ride-hailing have their own rules.
