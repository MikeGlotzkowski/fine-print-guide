How to read your policy

A policy is long, but it has a predictable structure. Start with the one-to-three page summary, the declarations page, and you'll know most of what matters in five minutes.

What a policy is made of

Declarations page
The summary: who and what is insured, the dates, each coverage with its limit, your deductibles, the price, and a list of the endorsements that apply. Start here.
Insuring agreement
The promise: what the insurer agrees to pay for, in broad terms.
Definitions
What the words mean in this policy. Words in bold or quotes in the policy are defined here, and the policy's definition wins over everyday meaning.
Exclusions
What is not covered. Read these carefully: most surprises live here.
Conditions
Your duties and the rules, like reporting claims promptly, protecting property after a loss, and how disputes are settled.
Endorsements
Changes added to the standard policy. They override the main text, so a policy can be broader or narrower than the standard form.

The declarations page, line by line

Here is a made-up homeowners declarations page. Yours will look different, but it will contain the same kinds of lines.

HOMEOWNERS POLICY DECLARATIONS Sample

1Named insuredAlex and Sam Rivera
2Policy periodJune 1, 2026 to June 1, 2027, 12:01 a.m.
3Policy formHO 00 03 (HO-3 Special Form)
4Coverage A, Dwelling$400,000
5Coverage B, Other structures$40,000
6Coverage C, Personal property$200,000, replacement cost
7Coverage D, Loss of use$80,000
8Coverage E, Personal liability$300,000 each occurrence
9Coverage F, Medical payments$5,000 each person
10Deductible, all perils$1,000
11Deductible, wind/hail2% of Coverage A ($8,000)
12EndorsementsHO 04 95 Water backup $10,000; HO 04 61 Scheduled jewelry
13MortgageeFirst Example Bank, loan #12345
14Annual premium$2,140
  1. Named insured. The people the policy covers. Relatives living with them are usually covered too. Anyone else, like a roommate, isn't unless added.
  2. Policy period. Coverage applies to losses that happen between these dates. On real US forms dates are written month first: 06/01/2026 is June 1.
  3. Policy form. The standard form it's based on. This tells you whether damage is covered by named or open perils. See the form comparison.
  4. Coverage A, Dwelling. The most it pays to rebuild the house. Check that it's enough to rebuild at today's prices.
  5. Coverage B, Other structures. Detached garage, fences and sheds. Here it's 10% of A, the usual default.
  6. Coverage C, Personal property. Your belongings, and how they're valued. "Replacement cost" is better than "actual cash value".
  7. Coverage D, Loss of use. Extra living costs if you have to move out during repairs.
  8. Coverage E, Personal liability. The most it pays for claims against you from one incident.
  9. Coverage F, Medical payments. Small medical bills for guests, whoever was at fault.
  10. Deductible, all perils. What you pay on each claim before the insurer pays.
  11. Deductible, wind/hail. A separate, larger deductible for wind and hail damage. Easy to miss, and common in storm-prone states.
  12. Endorsements. Add-ons that change the policy: here, sewer backup cover and a listed piece of jewelry. Look each one up in the policy packet.
  13. Mortgagee. Your lender. Claim payments for the house may be made out to you and the lender together.
  14. Annual premium. The price for the year. Discounts, like for alarms or bundling with car insurance, are often listed here.

Answering "am I covered?" in five steps

  1. Check the dates. Did the loss happen during the policy period?
  2. Check the property or person. Is what was damaged, or who was hurt, insured under this policy?
  3. Check the cause. Is the cause covered: listed in a named-perils policy, or not excluded in an open-perils one?
  4. Check the exclusions and endorsements. Does an exclusion take it away, or an endorsement add it back?
  5. Check the numbers. What are the limit, any sublimit, and the deductible for this kind of loss?

Questions worth asking your agent

  • Would my dwelling limit rebuild the house at today's prices?
  • Are my belongings paid at replacement cost or actual cash value?
  • Do I have a separate wind, hail or hurricane deductible, and how much is it in dollars?
  • Am I covered for sewer backup? For flood? For earthquake?
  • What are the limits for jewelry, electronics or anything valuable I own?
  • Is my liability limit enough, given what I own?
  • Can you send that in writing?

General information, not advice. Policies differ by insurer and by state. What your own policy says is what counts, so use this site to know what to look for and what to ask.
