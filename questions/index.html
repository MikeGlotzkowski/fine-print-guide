Common questions

Short answers to the questions people ask most about US insurance, with the reasons behind them.

Home and renting

  • Is renters insurance required?No law requires it, but many landlords do. If your lease says you need it, you have to buy it to keep the lease.
  • Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?No. Standard home and renters policies exclude flood. You need a separate flood policy, and it usually takes 30 days to start.
  • Does renters insurance cover things stolen outside my home?Usually yes. A standard renters policy covers your belongings almost anywhere, so a laptop stolen from a café, a bike taken from a rack or things stolen from your car are covered, minus your deductible.
  • Does my renters insurance cover my roommate?No, not unless they're named on your policy. Renters insurance covers you and relatives who live with you, so a roommate needs their own policy or to be added by name.
  • Does renters insurance cover water damage?It covers your belongings when water damage is sudden and comes from inside the building, like a burst pipe or an upstairs neighbor's overflowing tub. It doesn't cover flooding from outside or slow leaks.

Cars

  • Can I get car insurance with a foreign driver's license?Yes, some insurers will cover you, but you'll have fewer choices and pay more. Getting a US state license soon is the single biggest fix.
  • Does my car insurance cover a rental car?Usually yes, within the US and for personal use: your policy's coverage carries over to the rental. It covers damage to the rental only if you carry collision and comprehensive on your own car.
  • Does car insurance follow the car or the driver?Mostly the car. If you lend your car to a friend and they crash, your policy usually pays first. Their own car insurance may then pay on top if your limits run out.

Liability

  • What does umbrella insurance cover?Extra liability coverage on top of your home and car policies, usually $1 million or more, for when someone sues you for more than those policies pay.

Health

  • HMO vs PPO: what's the difference?An HMO covers care only from its own network and often needs a referral to see a specialist. A PPO lets you go outside the network for a higher share of the cost, and usually costs more each month.
  • I missed open enrollment. Can I still get health insurance?Only if a life event gives you a special enrollment period, like moving, losing other coverage, marriage or a new baby. Medicaid and CHIP accept people all year.
  • Is an explanation of benefits a bill?No. An explanation of benefits (EOB) is your health plan's summary of a claim. The bill comes separately from the doctor or hospital, and it should match what the EOB says you owe.
  • Is health insurance mandatory in the US?Not under federal law: there has been no federal penalty since 2019. But a few states require it, and some visas do too.
  • Do I need dental and vision insurance in the US?It's optional for adults. Most health plans don't cover adult dental care or glasses, so they're sold as separate, fairly small plans. Children's dental and vision are a standard benefit.
  • Does my health insurance work in another state?For emergencies, yes. For routine care, it depends on the plan type: HMO and EPO plans usually cover only their local network, while a PPO with a national network travels better.
  • What happens to my health insurance when I leave my job?Your job-based plan usually ends on your last day or at the end of that month. You can then keep it through COBRA at the full price, or pick a new plan on the Marketplace within 60 days.
  • What is an HSA, and can I open one?A health savings account is a tax-free savings account for medical costs. You can open one only if you're covered by a high-deductible health plan that qualifies, and have no other health coverage that disqualifies you.

Business

  • What insurance do I need as a freelancer?Health coverage first, since no employer provides it. After that, it depends on the work: professional liability if you give advice or build things for clients, general liability if you work at client sites, and cover for your equipment.
  • What is a certificate of insurance?A one-page summary that proves your business has insurance: which policies, with which insurer, the limits and the dates. Clients and landlords often ask for one before you start work.
  • Do I need workers' comp if I have only one employee?In most states, yes: workers' compensation is required as soon as you hire your first employee. A few states only require it above a small number of employees, and Texas lets most employers opt out.
  • Does my home insurance cover my home-based business?Barely. A home or renters policy usually covers business equipment only up to a small amount, often $2,500, and excludes liability for business activities. Most home businesses need an add-on or a small business policy.
  • Does my car insurance cover me if I use my car for work?Driving to and from work and the odd errand are usually covered. Deliveries, ride-hailing, carrying tools or goods, or a car owned by your business usually need extra or commercial coverage.

General information, not advice. Policies differ by insurer and by state. What your own policy says is what counts, so use this site to know what to look for and what to ask.
